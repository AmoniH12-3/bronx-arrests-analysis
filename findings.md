# Findings: Bronx arrests, 2006–2026

Each finding follows the same structure: what the data shows, how it compares, when it changed, and the caveat a reader needs.

## 1. Arrests shifted toward serious offenses (headline)

- Felonies were **22.4%** of Bronx arrests at their low in 2011 and **43.8%** at their peak in 2021. In 2025 they were **39.9%**.
- The biggest shift came between 2015 (25.9%) and 2021 (43.8%).
- This happened mostly because low-level arrests fell faster than felony arrests, not because felonies alone surged.
- **Why it matters:** each arrest increasingly involves a felony, which may mean more prosecutorial work per case even as total volume is lower.

## 2. Total arrests fell by two-thirds, then partly rebounded

- Arrests peaked at **100,535 in 2010**, fell to **32,724 in 2020** (−67%), and recovered to **62,833 in 2025**.
- 2025 is still about **38% below** the 2010 peak, but nearly double 2020.
- Misdemeanors drove most of the change: **75,393** (2010) → **18,538** (2020) → **37,000** (2025).
- **Caveat:** 2020 was shaped by the pandemic and should be read as an outlier.

## 3. Felony arrests are at a near-20-year high

- **25,077 felony arrests in 2025**, the most since 2007 (25,402) and **43% above 2019** (17,579).
- 2020 saw the largest one-year felony drop in the period (−21.7%).
- **Worth examining next:** which offenses and precincts account for the increase.

## 4. Drug arrests went from the largest category to a small one

- Dangerous-drug offenses were **35.2%** of arrests in 2007 and **5.6%** in 2022.
- In counts: **33,668 → 2,463**, a decline of about 93%.
- The decline started around **2012**, nearly a decade before marijuana legalization in 2021, so earlier enforcement and policy changes likely explain most of it.
- Since 2022 the share has risen slightly, to **8.2%** in 2025.
- **Next step:** split drug arrests into marijuana and other charges (`pd_desc`) to test how much of the decline was marijuana enforcement.

## 5. The Bronx has the highest arrest rate per resident

| Borough (2025) | Arrests | Per 100,000 residents |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bronx | 62,833 | 4,266.7 |
| Manhattan | 66,448 | 3,922.0 |
| Brooklyn | 79,882 | 2,919.6 |
| Queens | 58,083 | 2,414.6 |
| Staten Island | 11,707 | 2,361.5 |

- The Bronx rate is **46% higher than Brooklyn's** and **81% higher than Staten Island's**, even though Brooklyn had more total arrests.
- The Bronx is **16.7% of NYC's population** but accounted for **22.5% of 2025 arrests**.
- The Bronx or Manhattan has ranked first every year since 2006. The Bronx has ranked first every year since 2022, and the gap has widened.
- **Caveat:** Manhattan's per-resident rate is inflated by commuters and visitors who are arrested there but live elsewhere.

## 6. Arrests concentrate in the same precincts

- Precincts **46, 44, 40, 43 and 52** were the top five in both 2006 and 2007, only in a different order.
- **To confirm:** whether this group holds across all 20 years.

## Overall caveats

- Arrests measure enforcement as well as crime. These findings describe patterns; they do not establish causes.
- 2026 covers January–June only.
- Rates use 2020 population for every year.
